Project 04 · Stakeholder analysis

£200k to raise.
150+ doors to knock on.

Cycle of Life needed £200k in its first year to launch On the Go, a 12 week programme that helps disengaged young people in Liverpool build skills and run small businesses from food and drink bikes. As part of an MSc consultancy team, I built the research that showed who could fund it and who could partner with it.

01 · The problem

A new programme with no funding track record

The client had a strong idea and a deadline. What it didn't have was a clear view of who to ask for money, support and space.

£200kNeeded in year one

At least £75k to £100k just to run the first 12 week programme, and £200k by September 2024.

UnknownLow recognition

No official website and no track record to show funders what the money would achieve.

CrowdedStrong competition

Established programmes such as The Prince's Trust compete for the same funders and young people.

02 · The funding maths

To raise £88k, ask for £440k

Most applications fail, so the plan had to be built on success rates, not on the target alone. Our team turned published success rates into how much the client needed to apply for.

Grants20% success rate
Apply for£440k

Crowdfunding41% success rate

About 9% of the £206k start up budget

"A £200k target is really a question of how many applications you can send, and to whom."

03 · Execution

How I did it, in three moves

01

Gathered requirements

Turned client meetings into a research plan: funders, delivery partners, referral routes and storage for the bikes, each with a deadline.

02

Mapped stakeholders

Built a tracker of 150+ organisations in 13 categories, filtered by eligibility and sorted by the client's priorities, with a contact plan for each.

03

Appraised funding options

Matched 12 local trusts and foundations, offering grants from £2,000 to £100,000, to the programme's aims: young people, mental health, clean air and marginalised communities.

05 · Recommendations

What we handed over

01

A funding mix

Grants lead in year one at 50% of income, falling to 10% by year five, when sponsorship and contracts with schools and agencies bring in 80%.

02

Appeal to CSR goals

Pitch corporates on what they already report on: youth, crime reduction and the UK's net zero target.

03

A website that asks

One place to volunteer, donate, sponsor, give through payroll, leave a gift or become a digital ambassador.

06 · Next steps

What I'd do next

Track

Log the outcome of every application, so the client's own success rate replaces the 20% benchmark.

Rank

Rank funders by fit and grant size, and apply to the best matches first.

Own

Give every stakeholder in the tracker a named owner and a review date.
